### Human influence

[![World map of temperature distribution shows the northern hemisphere was warmer than the southern hemisphere during the periods compared.](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/e0/Change_in_Average_Temperature_With_Fahrenheit.svg/960px-Change_in_Average_Temperature_With_Fahrenheit.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Change_in_Average_Temperature_With_Fahrenheit.svg)

Surface air temperature change over the past 50 years[\[71\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-71)

See also: [Effects of climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change "Effects of climate change")
, [Effects of climate change on the water cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_climate_change_on_the_water_cycle "Effects of climate change on the water cycle")
, and [Urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island "Urban heat island")

The fine particulate matter produced by car exhaust and other human sources of pollution forms [cloud condensation nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_condensation_nuclei "Cloud condensation nuclei")
 leads to the production of clouds and increases the likelihood of rain. As commuters and commercial traffic cause pollution to build up over the course of the week, the likelihood of rain increases: it peaks by Saturday, after five days of weekday pollution has been built up. In heavily populated areas that are near the coast, such as the United States' [Eastern Seaboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_of_the_United_States "East Coast of the United States")
, the effect can be dramatic: there is a 22% higher chance of rain on Saturdays than on Mondays.[\[72\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-72)
 The urban heat island effect warms cities 0.6 to 5.6 °C (33.1 to 42.1 °F) above surrounding suburbs and rural areas. This extra heat leads to greater upward motion, which can induce additional shower and thunderstorm activity. Rainfall rates downwind of cities are increased between 48% and 116%. Partly as a result of this warming, monthly rainfall is about 28% greater between 32 and 64 km (20 and 40 mi) downwind of cities, compared with upwind.[\[73\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-73)
 Some cities induce a total precipitation increase of 51%.[\[74\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-74)

Increasing temperatures tend to increase evaporation which can lead to more precipitation. Precipitation generally increased over land north of 30°N from 1900 through 2005 but has declined over the tropics since the 1970s. Globally there has been no statistically significant overall trend in precipitation over the past century, although trends have varied widely by region and over time. Eastern portions of North and South America, northern Europe, and northern and central Asia have become wetter. The Sahel, the Mediterranean, southern Africa and parts of southern Asia have become drier. There has been an increase in the number of heavy precipitation events over many areas during the past century, as well as an increase since the 1970s in the prevalence of droughts—especially in the tropics and subtropics. Changes in precipitation and evaporation over the oceans are suggested by the decreased salinity of mid- and high-latitude waters (implying more precipitation), along with increased salinity in lower latitudes (implying less precipitation and/or more evaporation). Over the contiguous United States, total annual precipitation increased at an average rate of 6.1 percent since 1900, with the greatest increases within the East North Central climate region (11.6 percent per century) and the South (11.1 percent). Hawaii was the only region to show a decrease (−9.25 percent).[\[75\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-75)

Analysis of 65 years of United States of America rainfall records show the lower 48 states have an increase in heavy downpours since 1950. The largest increases are in the Northeast and Midwest, which in the past decade, have seen 31 and 16 percent more heavy downpours compared to the 1950s. [Rhode Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island "Rhode Island")
 is the state with the largest increase, 104%. [McAllen, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McAllen,_Texas "McAllen, Texas")
 is the city with the largest increase, 700%. Heavy downpour in the analysis are the days where total precipitation exceeded the top one percent of all rain and snow days during the years 1950–2014.[\[76\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-76)
[\[77\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-77)

The most successful attempts at influencing weather involve [cloud seeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_seeding "Cloud seeding")
, which include techniques used to increase [winter precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow "Snow")
 over mountains and suppress [hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail "Hail")
.[\[78\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-AMSmod-78)

### Acidity

[![](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b1/Origins_of_acid_rain.svg/960px-Origins_of_acid_rain.svg.png?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org&utm_campaign=parser&utm_content=thumbnail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Origins_of_acid_rain.svg)

Sources of acid rain

See also: [Acid rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acid_rain "Acid rain")

The phrase _acid rain_ was first used by Scottish chemist Robert Augus Smith in 1852.[\[90\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-90)
 The [pH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH "PH")
 of rain varies, especially due to its origin. On America's East Coast, rain that is derived from the Atlantic Ocean typically has a pH of 5.0–5.6; rain that comes across the continental from the west has a pH of 3.8–4.8; and local thunderstorms can have a pH as low as 2.0.[\[91\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-91)
 Rain becomes acidic primarily due to the presence of two strong acids, [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid "Sulfuric acid")
 (H2SO4) and [nitric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitric_acid "Nitric acid")
 (HNO3). Sulfuric acid is derived from natural sources such as volcanoes, and wetlands (sulfate-reducing bacteria); and anthropogenic sources such as the combustion of [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels "Fossil fuels")
, and mining where H2S is present. Nitric acid is produced by natural sources such as lightning, soil bacteria, and natural fires; while also produced anthropogenically by the combustion of fossil fuels and from power plants. In the past 20 years, the concentrations of nitric and sulfuric acid has decreased in presence of rainwater, which may be due to the significant increase in ammonium (most likely as ammonia from livestock production), which acts as a [buffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffer_solution "Buffer solution")
 in acid rain and raises the pH.[\[92\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-92)

### Pollution and composition

Aside from contamination of rainwater by [sulfuric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur(IV)_oxide "Sulfur(IV) oxide")
 and [nitric oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitric_oxides "Nitric oxides")
, which produces acid rain, various pollutants from industry and household wastes can end up in rainwater, causing detrimental effects on aquatic and human life. Pollutants from solid waste, leaking vehicles and machinery, fertilizers and other potentially hazardous substances enter water sources directly through dumping or as [surface runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_runoff "Surface runoff")
 following heavy rain.[\[103\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-103)
 One classification of pollutants of particular note is [perfluoroalkyl substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfluoroalkyl_acid "Perfluoroalkyl acid")
, synthetic chemical compounds used in a wide variety of consumer products.[\[104\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-Evidence-104)
 Rain has the potential to dissolve and transport different compounds, including the aforementioned toxic substances, with certain ions such as [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_ion "Calcium ion")
 and [bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicarbonate "Bicarbonate")
 appearing frequently, in more acidic rainwater.[\[105\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-105)
 However, the composition of rainwater at any given time and place is highly variable based on the ongoing manufacturing, farming, and waste-producing activities.[\[106\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-106)

.\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=PFAS&action=edit)\
\]

In 2022, levels of at least four perfluoroalkyl acids (PFAAs) in rain water worldwide greatly exceeded the EPA's lifetime [drinking water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_water "Drinking water")
 health advisories as well as comparable Danish, Dutch, and [European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union "European Union")
 safety standards, leading to the conclusion that "the global spread of these four PFAAs in the atmosphere has led to the [planetary boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_boundary "Planetary boundary")
 for chemical pollution being exceeded".[\[107\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-107)
 The most common PFAS found in the environment is [Trifluoroacetic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trifluoroacetic_acid "Trifluoroacetic acid")
 (TFA).[\[108\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-108)
 Its presence is ubiquitous in the environment, especially in aquatic ecosystems, where it persists with increasing concentrations globally.[\[109\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-109)

It had been thought that PFAAs would eventually end up in the oceans, where they would be diluted over decades, but a field study published in 2021 by researchers at [Stockholm University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_University "Stockholm University")
 found that they are often transferred from water to air when waves reach land, are a significant source of [air pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_pollution "Air pollution")
, and eventually get into rain. The researchers concluded that pollution may impact large areas.[\[110\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-110)
[\[111\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-111)
[\[112\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-112)
 Soil is also contaminated and the chemicals have been found in remote areas such as [Antarctica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctica "Antarctica")
.[\[113\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-113)
 [Soil contamination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_contamination "Soil contamination")
 can result in higher levels of PFAS found in foods such as white rice, coffee, and animals reared on contaminated ground.[\[114\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-114)
[\[115\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-PFAS_auto-115)
[\[116\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-116)
 In 2024, a worldwide study of 45,000 groundwater samples found that 31% of samples contained levels of PFAS that were harmful to human health; these samples were taken from areas not near any obvious source of contamination.[\[117\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-117)

Contamination has also been seen in water wells and other sources of drinking water. This contamination is seen in US, United Kingdom, Germany, Japan, and Canada, but information from most developing countries is nearly nonexistent.[\[118\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-118)
 The lack of information on PFAS contamination in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_country "Developing country")
, especially those in Africa, are due to structural socioeconomic inequality and lack of access to the expensive laboratory capabilities required for PFAS quantification.[\[119\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain#cite_note-PFAS_Aborode-2025-119)
